The full pipeline

HandCDO searches a single parametric design space covering palm shape, finger layout, kinematics, fingertips, and contact-surface curvature. Each candidate is scored in simulation, and the best designs are printed, assembled, and tested on real tool use.

Hover or tap a stage.

Abstract

Despite advances in dexterous hand manipulation, robotic hand design is still largely decoupled from task-driven evaluation and control, limiting systematic optimization. Existing robotic hand co-design approaches are often limited in scope, optimizing a small subset of design parameters. We introduce a comprehensive parametric framework for robotic hand generation that unifies palm structure, finger kinematics, fingertip geometry, and fine-scale surface curvatures within a single design space. Fine geometric features are introduced through parametric surface deformation kernels that directly influence contact interactions. We validate the framework on design optimization in grasp stability tasks in simulation and real-world dynamic scenarios. Our framework produces simulation- and fabrication-ready hand models, enabling rapid design iteration for dexterous hand co-design optimization and cross-embodiment policy training and control research.

How the co-design loop works

An outer optimizer proposes hands; an inner grasp search finds out how well each one can hold the task’s tool.

  1. A

    A tree-structured Parzen estimator (TPE) proposes the next hand: finger number, angles, offsets, link lengths, finger and thumb codes, fingertip size, and surface kernels. Past high and low scores shape where it samples next.

  2. B

    The parametric generator turns those numbers into meshes and a URDF, ready for simulation and for fabrication.

  3. Task

    Each task starts from a demonstrated power grasp and tool trajectory, recorded with the object’s 6-D pose. These define what the hand must hold and how the tool will move.

  4. C

    An inner search samples where the hand meets the tool, around the demonstrated grasp.

  5. D–E

    For each pose, joint configurations are sampled and the grasp is initialized in simulation.

  6. F

    The grasp is tested against disturbances along and about each axis. That score returns to the outer optimizer, which proposes the next hand.

Palm geometry

Shape, size, aspect ratio, orientation, and boundary smoothing define the palm outline.

Digit placement and count

Fingers and the thumb can sit anywhere along the palm outline, and where each one sits is itself a design parameter, as is the number of fingers.

Finger base offsets

Each finger moves independently along its attachment normal.

Finger base orientation

Outer fingers rotate outward and the thumb rotates, changing the hand’s spread.

Independent link lengths

Each link of each finger and the thumb can be extended on its own.

Joint types and topology

Digits cycle through rotational joint configurations as joints are added and removed.

Surface deformation

Deformation kernels vary height, width, aspect, position, and orientation on palm and finger pads.

Fingertip dimensions

Each fingertip scales independently in width, depth, and length.
